How to Play

  • After logging in you land on the Main Menu: create a new table (optionally named, optionally password-protected — 🔒 in the list means it needs one) or join one already open, each fully separate from the others — your board, life, decks-in-play, chat, and voice are all scoped to the table you're at. My Decks (also on the Main Menu) is where your saved decks live — Edit opens one in the Deck Editor, Del removes it. Leave Table (⚙ Menu, in-game) returns to the menu — your seat (board, hand, library, commander, everything) is held for 60 seconds in case you rejoin the same table, shown as a countdown next to your name in the Players panel; after that it's freed up for good.
  • Spectate instead of Join to watch a table without taking a seat — you see the board and life totals like any non-owner (hands and face-down cards stay hidden), can use chat and voice, but can't touch cards or start/advance the game. Works on a game already in progress, not just a fresh table.
  • The Deck Editor (Main Menu, or ⚙ Menu once in a table) is where you build a deck: up to 2 commander slots and a 99-card library, either added one at a time or bulk-pasted as a decklist — commanders go in their own slots so they never end up shuffled into the library. Loading a saved deck auto-fills your commander slots too, and its own "Load Into Current Game" button is how you swap decks mid-game.
  • Every card belongs to a zone (Lands/Mana, Creatures, Artifacts & Enchantments, Hand) based on its real type, and lays itself out automatically.
  • Drag your own card into a different row to move/play it. Right-click a hand card → Play to Battlefield does the same instantly. Dragging a permanent back into the Hand row does nothing on purpose — there's no general rule that lets you just pick a permanent back up. To represent a bounce effect, right-click the permanent and choose Return to Hand instead.
  • Playing a land is capped at one per turn (see "Land drops" in the sidebar; use +1 extra drop for effects that let you play more). Once the game has started, lands and anything else that isn't an Instant (or doesn't have Flash) can only be played on your own turn, during a main phase — Instants and Flash cards can go anytime.
  • Playing anything else checks your mana pool against the card's real cost — you need the mana or it won't go through. This includes casting your Commander from its zone: it costs its real mana cost plus the current commander tax shown there, and it's disabled while a copy of it is already on the battlefield.
  • Tap a basic land, or any land/rock/dork archived with exactly one fixed color, to auto-add its color to your pool. A source that can produce more than one color (Command Tower, most signets/talismans, City of Brass) prompts you to pick instead of guessing or staying fully manual. The manual +/− buttons in the Mana Pool panel are still there for anything else — use − to back out a misclick. Mana empties at the end of every step/phase, like the real rules. A land with "enters the battlefield tapped" in its unconditional text (not shocklands/checklands, which involve a real choice) now actually enters tapped.
  • Double-click your own card to tap it (using it, e.g. for mana) — this only ever taps, since there's no real rule that lets you freely untap something at will; untapping only happens automatically each Untap step, or via Untap All for effects that untap things. Right-click for the full action menu — only your own cards can be tapped/flipped/countered/removed; anyone can Target anything.
  • A creature has summoning sickness (shown tilted) until it's been under your control since the start of your most recent turn — it can still block, just not attack, unless it has Haste.
  • Opening Hand pops up automatically the moment a game starts: Draw 7, Mulligan up to twice, then Keep Hand — stays up until you keep a hand, then it's gone for the rest of the game.
  • Hand size is capped at 7: ending your turn (past End Step) with more than that opens a required discard prompt — pick exactly enough cards to get back to 7 before the turn actually ends. A permanent whose text says "no maximum hand size" exempts you entirely.
  • Casting a spell (anything except a land) puts it on the Stack instead of resolving instantly — everyone gets a real window to respond with an instant/flash spell of their own before it resolves, last-in-first-out, same as real Magic. Whoever currently has priority sees a "You have priority" prompt with a Pass button; once everyone passes in a row, the top of the stack resolves — a permanent goes to the battlefield, an instant/sorcery goes to the graveyard. You can't play a land, advance the turn, or move combat forward while something's pending. What a spell's text actually *does* is still up to you to adjudicate manually, same as combat keywords — the stack only handles the sequencing. Any player can hit Counter on a stack item to send it straight to the graveyard (or, for a ⚡ triggered ability, just remove it), representing a counterspell or similar effect that resolved elsewhere.
  • A small, growing list of specific cards have a real triggered ability ("when this enters the battlefield...") automated — it shows up on the Stack marked ⚡, resolves the same way a spell does, and actually applies its effect (draws cards, creates tokens, changes life totals, destroys/exiles/bounces a chosen creature) when it resolves. This only covers a card's own trigger about itself, hand-picked and added over time — everything else about every card, and every card without one of these, is still adjudicated manually exactly as before. A few of these need a target — a gold banner appears telling you what to pick, and clicking any card on the battlefield chooses it (the trigger doesn't even join the Stack until you do).
  • Manage Keywords (right-click a battlefield permanent) lets you grant it Flying, Haste, Indestructible, and other common keywords, representing an aura/equipment/anthem effect — shown as small icon badges on the card. Nothing is enforced automatically except Haste, which already plugs into the summoning-sickness check.
  • Attach To... (right-click, on equipment/auras or anything else) links a card to another permanent — shown as a small "⚭ Host Name" label on the attached card, and "🔗 Name" on the host listing what's attached to it. If the card's text has a real Equip cost, it's paid automatically from your mana pool; anything else (like an aura, whose cost was already paid casting it) attaches for free. A simple, common pattern of oracle text — "Equipped/Enchanted creature gets +X/+Y" and/or "...has [keyword]" — is automated: the bonus and any granted keyword actually apply to combat math and summoning-sickness checks (shown as a small blue +X/+Y badge and a keyword icon on the card), for as long as the attachment lasts. Anything more conditional than that plain wording isn't picked up and still needs a manual Manage Keywords grant. If the host leaves the battlefield, an attached Aura goes to the graveyard with it; anything else just becomes unattached. Detach is always free, for manual correction.
  • Take Control (right-click any permanent that isn't already yours) represents an effect like Control Magic — the card moves to your board and you can play it as your own; it's marked "stolen from X" and treated as freshly under your control (summoning sick again, like real Magic). Return Control hands it back to its real owner whenever the effect should end — nothing tracks that duration automatically, same as everything else this app doesn't automate.
  • Create Copy (right-click any permanent) represents an effect like Clone — makes you a fresh token sharing that card's current printed name/type/P-T/text/keywords, not its counters, attachments, or any equipment bonus (matching how a real copy effect works). Fires that card's own "enters the battlefield" trigger if it has one, same as any other new permanent. Blocked on an opponent's face-down card, so it can't be used to peek at something hidden.
  • Life at 0 or below, 21+ combat damage from a single commander, or 10+ poison, eliminates a player — their board stays on the table (frozen, not removed) and they're taken out of the turn order for the rest of the game, but they can keep watching by switching board tabs. Once only one player is left standing, the game ends and everyone sees a win screen (a simultaneous knockout of everyone remaining ends in a draw instead) — leaving the table counts toward that too, not just elimination. Concede (⚙ Menu) does the same thing voluntarily, for when you know you're out and don't want to wait for it to happen — different from Leave Table, which actually removes your seat instead of just marking you eliminated.
  • Cards get a brief glow/pop-in when they enter the battlefield and fade out when they leave, attacking/blocking creatures pulse red/blue during combat, damage shows as a floating number (or a red flash on a player's row when it's life loss), and casting a spell gives it a quick golden pulse.
  • Free Cast (no cost) (right-click a hand card) represents an effect like Cascade or Through the Breach — casts it without paying its mana cost or checking whose turn it is, straight onto the stack like any other cast. A manual tool for whatever already-resolved effect earned it; nothing verifies you actually earned it.
  • Create Token (top bar) builds a token from scratch — name, type line, power/toughness, colors, and Flying/Haste checkboxes — since tokens aren't real cards and can't be looked up. A handful of common presets (Soldier, Zombie, Treasure, etc.) fill the form in one click.
  • Start Game rolls a d20 for each player to decide turn order (highest goes first), then begins the turn structure — only the active player sees Next Phase. Untap (auto-untaps + resets land drops), Upkeep, and Draw (auto-draws, skipped turn 1 for the starting player) all auto-advance straight through to Main 1 with a single click, since there's never a decision to make there. Combat auto-skips straight to Main 2 the same way if you have no creatures to attack with.
  • Combat: on your turn's Combat phase, click "Declare Attackers" to pick creatures and who each attacks. Defenders then get "Declare Blockers". Damage resolves automatically from real power/toughness (plus counters) — lethal damage sends a creature to the graveyard, unblocked damage hits the player. Commander damage is tracked per *commander*, not just a flat total — a breakdown appears under My Status listing each opposing commander that's hit you and how much, exactly like the real 21-damage-from-a-single-commander rule needs, and it stays even if that commander later leaves the battlefield. Note: this is vanilla combat math only — keyword abilities like deathtouch, first strike, or trample aren't automatically applied.
  • Board tabs switch whose board you're viewing. Library/Graveyard/Exile piles sit at the left of each board. Fit (next to the Zoom slider) auto-scales to frame the whole current board at once instead of eyeballing the slider.
  • Chat is in the sidebar; Voice Chat (experimental, peer-to-peer) is in the ⚙ Menu. Poison only shows up under My Status once it's actually relevant — an infect/toxic card in your own deck or anywhere visible on the battlefield.
  • Board Mat (⚙ Menu → Profile) sets a custom background image for your own board via image URL — Clear reverts to the default. Everyone viewing your board tab sees it. Save one under a name in the Account modal to reuse it on any table later instead of pasting the URL every time — pick it from the dropdown next to Set/Clear and hit Apply.
  • Account (Main Menu, or ⚙ Menu → Profile → Account Settings) is where account-wide preferences live, separate from the per-table nickname field: an avatar (by image URL, shown next to your color dot in the Players panel), a default nickname used every time you join a new table, and a change-password form. These persist across every table, unlike the per-table nickname override.
  • Hit something broken? The Bug Report button in the ⚙ Menu builds a copyable diagnostic report (recent game log + any client errors) — nothing is sent anywhere automatically, just copy it and hand it over.
